Among the art exhibitions to catch is Masaaki Sato: New York Since 1970, a retrospective celebrating the work of an artist who has spent more than fifty years finding the unexpected in the everyday sights of New York. Born in Kofu, Japan, in 1941, Sato studied in London before arriving in New York in 1970. Across paintings, prints, and sculpture, he transforms familiar subjects—from subway cars and newsstands to magazine covers and apples—into playful explorations of how we see and experience the world around us.

His Holes series introduces repeated conical openings into bodies, vehicles, and everyday spaces, disrupting the solidity of familiar forms. In his Newsstands works, headlines, faces, and magazine titles become material for visual invention. Sato's work balances meticulous observation with a mischievous impulse to challenge what seems familiar, inviting viewers to look again.
