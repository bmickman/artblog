From Tribeca and SoHo to the Lower East Side, Two Bridges, Chinatown, and the West Village, this week’s listings invite you to explore downtown Manhattan’s ever-evolving gallery scene. Discover contemporary painting, ambitious installations, thoughtful group exhibitions, and independent art spaces that offer new perspectives on the city’s creative landscape. Whether you’re planning a full afternoon of gallery hopping or looking for a single exhibition to inspire your week, there’s plenty to discover across these six neighborhoods.

As you make your way through downtown, take time to explore beyond the familiar gallery corridors. From the established spaces of Tribeca and SoHo to the independent and experimental venues of the Lower East Side, Two Bridges, and Chinatown, this week offers an invitation to slow down, encounter new work, and see the neighborhoods—and the art within them—with fresh eyes.

This week’s featured artist is Susan Jane Walp, whose new exhibition at Tibor de Nagy Gallery opens October 24. Marking her eighth solo presentation with the gallery, the exhibition brings together intimate still-life paintings that find extraordinary richness in everyday objects. Working with a familiar repertoire of fruit, glass bowls, cups, and ancient Greek vessels, Walp creates meticulously balanced compositions defined by shape, texture, and subtle shifts in color and mark-making. Repeated motifs become opportunities for fresh discoveries, transforming ordinary arrangements into meditative studies of perception, precision, and the quiet beauty of looking closely.
